# Ironroot: business case

**Status:** solo-built MVP, not launched. No users, revenue or funding. Phase two is paused pending funding. Every number in sections 4 and 5 is an **assumption to test**, not a result.

## 1. Problem
Underweight and lean young adults who want to build muscle hit three walls: gyms cost money, eating enough on a tight budget is hard to plan, and most people drop off within weeks. Existing tools usually solve one of these and paywall the rest.

## 2. Target user (hypothesis)
Aged 18 to 25, student or early career, no gym access or budget, wants to gain weight and muscle, spends £30 to £40 a week on food.

## 3. What exists today
A working MVP: 5-day home training plan with illustrated exercises, a rotating 28-day menu costed at £31.94 to £34.54 a week, and a streak tracker. It runs entirely in the browser.

| Validated | Not yet validated |
|---|---|
| The product works end to end | Willingness to pay |
| Menu hits calorie and protein targets across all 28 days | Whether the streak mechanic improves adherence |
| Menu fits a £30 to £40 weekly budget | Retention beyond the first few weeks |

## 4. Business model (hypothesis)
Freemium. **Free:** training plan, week-one menu, tracker. **Premium at £3.99 a month (illustrative):** full 4-week rotation, personalised plans, progress analytics.

## 5. Unit economics (illustrative assumptions)

The key unknown is retention. With a 3-month life, paid advertising does not pay back, so growth would have to be organic (short-form video, student communities).

## 6. Phase two roadmap
1. **Accounts:** sign-up and sign-in, with cloud sync.
2. **Onboarding questionnaire:** goal, height and weight, equipment, food budget, dietary needs, available days.
3. **Personalisation:** generate the training split and menu from the answers instead of one fixed plan.
4. **Validation first:** a landing page with a waitlist, 15 to 20 user conversations, then a 4-week pilot measuring week-4 retention before building anything expensive.

## 7. Risks
- **Health:** users who are underweight should see a GP. The app must flag very low BMI, avoid medical claims, and never encourage extreme restriction or overeating.
- **Data protection:** once accounts exist, health-related data falls under UK GDPR. The local-first MVP avoids this; phase two must handle it properly.
- **Competition:** free YouTube programmes and established fitness apps. The angle is the combination of home-only training and a costed budget menu.
- **Retention:** most fitness apps lose users fast. The streak mechanic is untested.

## 8. Funding
Not currently raising. Phase two resumes when time or funding allow, starting with the validation step above.
